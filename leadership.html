2026

Emergency Food Distribution for Communities Affected by Flash Floods

ASHIKA Development Associates, funded by the World Food Programme · Rangamati Hill District
  • Problem. Flooding hit five upazilas of Rangamati Hill District hard. Households needed food and energy supplies quickly, and there was no record yet of who was affected or what each family still lacked.
  • What I did. Helped distribute rice, oil, dry food, and energy biscuits under the relief programme funded by the World Food Programme. Together with two supporting team members, I collected household data from the affected families on paper forms and entered it afterwards, creating the first structured record of who was affected and what each family still lacked, so that relief could be directed by need.
  • Role. Volunteer and coordinator among the affected households, as part of a team led by Ratna Jyoti Chakma. I volunteered specifically in Rangamati Sadar.
  • Result. The distribution reached 396 households across five affected upazilas: Rangamati Sadar, Baghaichhari, Belaichhari, Barkal, and Juraichhari.

Indigenous Food Habits & Climate Change Awareness

Self-organized, with support from ASHIKA Development Associates · Kaptai Upazila (Rangamati) and Bandarban Sadar Upazila (Bandarban)
  • Programme. Awareness Programme on Indigenous Peoples' Food Habits and Climate-Driven Change in Food Habits.
  • Problem. Mothers and adolescent girls (kishories) in the Chittagong Hill Tracts had few structured sessions on how local food habits relate to nutrition and to climate change.
  • What I did. Organized and led a group of 15 volunteers, and went out to communities in Kaptai and Bandarban Sadar upazilas to meet mothers and adolescent girls (kishories). We held meetings and awareness sessions with them, including at UNICEF-supported Para Centres and a monthly cluster meeting of Para Centre workers. The sessions covered what the region's indigenous food habits offer for nutrition, and how climate change affects food and jhum cultivation. Printed material was produced from the sessions.
  • Leadership. Assembled and led the 15-person team myself, rather than joining one someone else had organized.
  • Result. The programme ran from 11 to 13 June 2026 and reached 48 mothers and kishories.
Show full write-up

The Chittagong Hill Tracts differ from the plains districts of Bangladesh in what people eat and how they farm. A published review of traditional food plants in the region describes indigenous livelihoods as still dominated by subsistence agriculture that combines jhum (shifting cultivation), plough cultivation, and mixed fruit gardens. The programme asked what this local food base offers for the nutrition of mothers and adolescent girls.

Climate change bears on the same food base. An ethnographic study in Kutukchhari and Sapchhari unions of Rangamati Sadar found that climate change and human causes together threaten food security and disrupt farming, and that people are being pushed to change their traditional jhum cultivation. In work reported by UNESCO, a youth-led study of 72 Tripura women in Simana Para, Khagrachari found that erratic weather disrupts jhum cultivation, water access, health, and food security.

Kaptai was chosen because it shows how loss of land changes food. The Kaptai Dam flooded about 22,000 ha, around 40% of the cultivable land in the Chittagong Hill Tracts, displaced about 100,000 people, most of them Chakma, and shortened jhum cycles from 7–10 years to 3–5.

Para Centres are neighbourhood centres set up by UNICEF with the Chittagong Hill Tracts Development Board under the Integrated Community Development Project, and now run under the Board's Sustainable Social Services Delivery Project. They deliver health, nutrition, education, and water and sanitation services to mothers and children in remote villages.

Participants discussed how food and climate change are linked, and how changes in jhum affect what is grown and eaten.

Sources: Garai (2025), Wild 2(2):13; UNESCO, youth-led climate research in the Chittagong Hill Tracts; Traditional food plants and agro-biodiversity in the Chittagong Hill Tracts; UNICEF, Many Tracts One Community; UNICEF, evaluation of the Integrated Community Development Project; Involuntary displacement and human security: the Kaptai Dam.
