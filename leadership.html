Leadership & Extracurricular Activities

I supervise two research interns at ELITE Research Lab. In 2023 and 2024 I organized and led a three-day workshop on food habits and climate change for 20 to 50 mothers and adolescent girls each time, and I handled the logistics of winter relief that reached about 60 families and 52 students.
2026

Emergency flash flood relief

ASHIKA Development Associates, funded by the World Food Programme · Rangamati
  • Problem. Flooding hit five upazilas of Rangamati Hill District hard. Households needed food and energy supplies quickly, and there was no record yet of who was affected or what each family still lacked.
  • What I did. Helped distribute rice, oil, dry food, and energy biscuits under the relief program funded by the World Food Programme. Together with two supporting team members, I collected household data from the affected families on paper forms and entered it afterwards, creating the first structured record of who was affected and what each family still lacked, so that relief could be directed by need.
  • Role. Volunteer and coordinator among the affected households, as part of a team led by Ratna Jyoti Chakma.
  • Result. The distribution reached about 400 households across five affected upazilas: Rangamati Sadar, Baghaichhari, Belaichhari, Barkal, and Juraichhari.

2024

AI seminar organizer & host

Independent initiative · Schools and colleges of Rangamati Hill District
  • Problem. Students at schools and colleges across Rangamati Hill District had little exposure to how AI systems work and where they fail.
  • What I did. Organized the seminar series myself, without an institutional sponsor, and ran the sessions with four or more fellow RMSTU students as co-presenters. The sessions covered AI applications, ethical considerations, and safety practices.
  • Leadership. Initiated the series, chose the schools and colleges to visit, and recruited the fellow students who co-presented with me.
  • Result. The series ran at six schools and colleges across the district, reaching more than 300 students in total.
  • Venues. Included Moanoghar Residential School and Moanoghar Pre-Cadet School, Rangamati Government College, Rangamati Government Mohila College, and Rani Dayamoyee High School.
Winters
2023, 2024

Winter relief distribution

RMSTU Indigenous Students' Family · Baghaichhari and Kaptai upazilas, Rangamati Hill District
  • Problem. Indigenous families in rural parts of Rangamati Hill District needed books, warm clothing, and study materials during winter.
  • What I did. As a general member and volunteer of the Family, I helped collect funds and used, clean winter clothes, distribute supplies, gather data on affected families, and coordinate logistics.
  • Leadership. Handled the on-the-ground logistics across both winters.
  • Result. We, the members of the RMSTU Indigenous Students' Family, including me, collected around BDT 45,000 across the two winters from RMSTU teachers and other community members of Rangamati Sadar, along with used, clean winter clothes. The distribution reached about 60 families in three villages, two in Baghaichhari Upazila and one in Kaptai Upazila, and 52 students received the study materials they needed.
2023, 2024

Food habits & climate change workshop

Self-organized · Chittagong Hill Tracts
  • Problem. Mothers and adolescent girls (kishories) in the Chittagong Hill Tracts had few structured sessions on how local food habits relate to nutrition and to climate change.
  • What I did. Organized and led a group of 15 volunteers to run a three-day workshop on the food habits of the Chittagong Hill Tracts and the impact of climate change for mothers and adolescent girls (kishories). The sessions covered what the region's indigenous food habits offer for nutrition, and how climate change affects food and jhum cultivation. Printed material was produced from the sessions.
  • Leadership. Assembled and led the 15-person team myself, rather than joining one someone else had organized.
  • Result. The workshop ran across 2023 and 2024, with 20 to 50 mothers and kishories attending each time.
