An Ensemble Approach for Diabetic Retinopathy Severity Classification with Diffusion-Based Augmentation and Explainability

Auntor Chakma, Diya Rahman, T. H. Liew

Accepted 9 August 2026

Show abstract

Public fundus-image datasets are dominated by normal and mild cases, so models trained on them read common stages well but miss advanced disease, the stages where a missed reading matters most. This paper treats that failure as four separate problems rather than one: too few examples of rare stages, a severity scale that ordinary classifiers treat as unrelated categories, no way to check where a model is actually looking, and single models that share the same blind spots. Each gets its own fix: Stable Diffusion 2.1 with ControlNet generates additional minority-class images, an ordinal loss teaches the model that stages progress rather than sit as unrelated labels, lesion masks direct attention during training rather than only explaining it afterward, and three architecturally different backbones are combined through a learned meta-learner instead of a fixed voting rule. On 1,437 fundus photographs from Hospital de Clínicas, Paraguay, the combination reaches 91.63% accuracy and an AUC of 0.9920 on held-out images, cuts the ordinal model's mean error from 0.78 to 0.46 stages, and, at matched training-set size, beats standard image augmentation by 4.65% absolute accuracy. To our knowledge, no prior published pipeline combines all four elements for seven-stage classification, and removing any one of them measurably hurt accuracy.

Multimodal Federated Learning with Privacy-Preserving Mechanisms in Healthcare: A Mini Review of Convergence, Challenges, and Future Directions

Auntor Chakma, F. T. Z. Moon, F. Jahan, M. K. Morol, T. H. Liew, D. Nandi, A. A. Jubair, M. Rahman

Accepted September 2026. My role: first author, from the literature search to the written review, during a three-month internship at ELITE Research Lab.

Show abstract

Hospitals hold scan images, health records, and genomic data that improve diagnosis when combined, but each data type carries a different amount of patient-identifiable information, and most federated systems protect all of them with a single shared privacy setting. This mini-review searched seven databases and examined 14 studies published between 2020 and early 2026 (eight reporting original results and six providing field-level context) to find out how far the field has actually gone. Federated training proved stable at real scale, with one glioblastoma imaging federation holding steady across 71 hospitals, and multimodal models beat single-modality ones in most direct comparisons reported. Privacy design told a different story. Only 1 of the 8 empirical studies (12.5%) applied a formal privacy mechanism to model updates at all, and none of them gave different data types their own privacy protection, so a scan and a heart-rate reading were treated the same. The review reports this as an open question rather than a proven flaw.

A Deep Learning–Based Framework for Bangla Vehicle License Plate Detection and Recognition

Sagar Mohajan, G. M. Sakhawat Hossain, Md Mynoddin, Rishita Chakma, Tanjim Mahmud, Punam Kanungoe, Auntor Chakma

Published 10 January 2026. My role: model development, and writing and figures.

Show abstract

Automated license-plate recognition built for Latin scripts performs poorly on Bangla plates, where character shapes, ligatures, and inconsistent plate quality across Bangladeshi vehicles break the assumptions those systems rely on. This work builds an end-to-end pipeline specifically for that script: an image-restoration stage first corrects the blur, low light, and motion artefacts typical of roadside camera captures, and a sequence-recognition model then reads the restored plate as an ordered character sequence rather than a set of isolated glyphs. On a synthetic Bangla license-plate dataset built to reflect that variability, the pipeline reaches 96% character-level accuracy, giving a practical basis for local vehicle-identification systems that existing Latin-script recognizers cannot serve.

Visual Prompt Injection in Multimodal AI Agents

Auntor Chakma, mentored by Md Kishor Morol

Early-stage project on how vision-language agents can be steered by instructions hidden in images.

Show description

AI agents that read screens, web pages, and documents through a vision-language model can be steered by instructions hidden in an image, such as text in a banner, an ad, or a screenshot, that the user never sees as a command. This project measures how often open vision-language agents follow such injected instructions, which kinds of visual injection succeed, and whether the model's attention singles out the injected region before the agent acts. The aim is a benchmark of realistic injections and a lightweight check that flags a compromised screen before an action is taken.

Signal Processing on Medical Equipment for Outcome Prediction

Auntor Chakma (supervising), with two research interns

Combines the ICBHI, KAUH and RD@TR lung-sound datasets and extends Pulmo-TS2ONN (IEEE TIM 2024) into one model that classifies pulmonary disorders from respiratory sounds. The goals are low computational cost and predictions that come with an explanation, so that a health worker in a low-resource clinic can use the model. The work began in August 2026, and the target venue is a Springer Nature journal.

Attentive TabNet Framework for Multiclass Intrusion Detection in Internet of Medical Things (IoMT) Environments

Auntor Chakma, B.Sc. thesis, Rangamati Science and Technology University · Supervisor: Md Mynoddin

An attention-based TabNet framework with conditional-GAN synthetic samples for rare attack classes, reaching 99.6% accuracy on 2 classes, 97.9% on 6 categories and 95.7% on 19 attack types.

Show description

An attention-based TabNet framework that handles three detection tasks in one model, with conditional-GAN synthetic samples for rare attack classes. On CICIoMT2024 (8,775,013 raw records from 40 devices), it reaches 99.6% accuracy on 2 classes, 97.9% on 6 categories and 95.7% on 19 attack types, beating Random Forest, XGBoost and Logistic Regression on every task. GAN balancing beat SMOTE on all three, and the gap widened as the task got harder.
